01

Machine Learning · Data Mining

Predicting Controllable Flight Delays

How can airlines identify flights at higher risk for delays they may actually be able to influence?

Click any project image to open it at full size.

Data2024 U.S. BTS domestic departures from North Carolina
MethodsDecision Tree · Random Forest · Logistic Regression · XGBoost · SHAP
FocusClassification · imbalance · explainability

The problem

My team focused on delays connected to carrier operations, NAS congestion and security rather than weather or late arriving aircraft. We engineered variables around departure time, weekends, carriers and airports and prepared categorical features for modeling.

What made it interesting

The delayed flight class was imbalanced, so a model could look good on accuracy while missing the flights we actually cared about. We compared recall, balanced accuracy and F1 alongside accuracy. Scaled XGBoost gave us the strongest overall balance among the models we tested.

What I took from it

A high accuracy score is not automatically a useful model. The evaluation metric has to match the decision the model is supposed to support.

From model to decision

SHAP helped us interpret predictions instead of treating the model as a black box. Departure hour emerged as the strongest predictor, followed by factors including carrier, week of year, weekends and holidays, and Charlotte Douglas International Airport. We connected those findings to proactive scheduling and resource allocation.

02

Independent Research · SURF

Parallel Paths to Crime: How Internal Traits Shape Fraud Motivation

Why do people facing similar opportunities and pressures make very different choices about occupational fraud?

RoleFirst author · research under Dr. Ifeoma Udeh
ApproachInterdisciplinary literature review · conceptual model
FieldsFraud · psychology · behavioral ethics · criminology

From a class idea to independent research

This project began in my Financial Crimes capstone. I kept coming back to a gap in traditional fraud frameworks: opportunity and pressure can explain the circumstances, but not why people in similar circumstances make very different choices. I developed that question into independent research and a first authored SURF poster.

The framework

I synthesized theoretical literature, empirical studies, offender accounts and case evidence around recurring psychological stages. The resulting dual path framework proposes different internal routes into fraud that converge through repeated rationalization toward moral disengagement and emotional distancing.

The idea I wanted to add

The framework treats rationalization as an ongoing developmental mechanism rather than only an explanation someone gives after the fact.

03

Machine Learning · Animal Welfare

Austin Animal Center Adoption Patterns

What characteristics are associated with dog adoption outcomes in a large, messy shelter dataset?

Click any project image to open it at full size.

Preparing real shelter data

Our team cleaned and preprocessed years of Austin Animal Center records, handled missing values and date fields, explored age, sex and breed patterns, and created variables suitable for modeling. I worked directly on reducing the shelter's many breed labels into meaningful analytical categories and translating those rules into a Python mapping structure.

Interpreting the model

We evaluated logistic regression with recall, precision, specificity and F1, not accuracy alone. We connected the results to practical animal welfare recommendations around older dogs, spay and neuter status, and reducing overemphasis on breed.

Why this one feels very me

It combines a technical modeling problem with animal welfare. The cleaning and feature engineering decisions felt consequential because the data represented real shelter outcomes.

04

Psychology Capstone · Experimental Research

Reinforcing the Untrainable

Can an ABA based positive reinforcement intervention increase trained behaviors in a domestic cat?

DesignSingle subject AB experiment
Timeline10 day baseline · 24 day intervention
BehaviorsStay · back up · scratch

Designing the study

For my Psychology empirical research capstone, I designed and carried out a repeated measures behavioral experiment with my cat, Kisses. I operationalized correct responses, randomized cue order, collected daily data and used cooperative care principles so participation remained voluntary.

Results that were not neat

Overall correct responses increased from a 0% baseline average to 41% during intervention. Stay averaged 70% correct and back up 54%, while scratch remained at 0%. Instead of hiding the unsuccessful outcome, I investigated what made scratching different, including intrinsic and competing reinforcement.

My biggest takeaway

Real research data is not always pretty, consistent or expected. An unexpected result is a reason to investigate, not a reason to force the original hypothesis.

Research ethics in practice

When Kisses developed a serious UTI during the study, I paused data collection for her welfare. A clean dataset never matters more than the subject behind it.

05

Product · AI · Pet Health

Pawfect Care

How do you turn scattered daily pet health observations into something actually useful?

From class prototype to a problem I actually care about

The original class concept centralized pet care tracking, reminders, appointments and records. I kept developing it because the problem became much more concrete to me: individual measurements are less useful without context, history and patterns.

Where I want to take it

The newer wireframes add customizable health trackers, chronological events, respiratory rate logging, water and litter box trends, context filters, combined measurements and pattern detection. The goal is not to replace veterinary judgment. I want it to make an owner's observations more structured and easier to interpret and share.

Product principle

Smarter data should make care feel clearer, not create another dashboard you have to babysit.

I/O Psychology · Workplace Behavior

Why Good Employees Do Bad Things

What drives counterproductive work behavior, and what can organizations actually do about it?

I synthesized organizational psychology research on individual traits, workplace stressors, organizational justice and ethical climate. Using the Stressor Emotion Model, I examined how systems and emotions can contribute to counterproductive behavior rather than reducing the problem to simply having “bad employees.”

Connection to People Analytics

This reinforced my interest in workplace outcomes as an interaction between people and systems, and in using evidence to move from “what happened?” to “what could we change?”
