Predicting Controllable Flight Delays

How can airlines identify flights at higher risk for delays they may actually be able to influence?

The problem

My team created a target focused on controllable delays—carrier, NAS congestion, and security—rather than weather and late-arriving-aircraft delays. We engineered variables around departure time, weekends, carriers and airports and prepared categorical features for modeling.

What made it interesting

The delayed-flight class was imbalanced, so a model could look good on accuracy while missing the flights we cared about. We compared recall, balanced accuracy and F1 alongside accuracy. Scaled XGBoost produced the strongest overall balance among the models we tested.

Analytical judgment

A high accuracy score is not automatically a useful model. The evaluation metric has to match the decision the model is supposed to support.

From model to decision

SHAP helped us interpret predictions instead of treating the model as a black box. Departure hour emerged as the strongest predictor, followed by factors including carrier, week of year, weekends/holidays and Charlotte Douglas International Airport. We translated that into recommendations around proactive scheduling and resource allocation.

Parallel Paths to Crime: How Internal Traits Shape Fraud Motivation

Why do people facing similar opportunities and pressures make very different choices about occupational fraud?

Moral disengagement → emotional distancing / numbing

ApproachInterdisciplinary literature review + conceptual model
FieldsFraud · psychology · behavioral ethics · criminology

From class idea to independent research

The project began in a Financial Crimes capstone. I was interested in a gap in traditional fraud frameworks: opportunity and pressure help explain circumstances, but not why people in similar circumstances respond differently. I developed that question into independent research and a first-authored SURF poster.

The framework

I synthesized theoretical literature, empirical studies, offender accounts and case evidence around recurring psychological stages. The resulting dual-path framework proposes different internal routes into fraud that converge through repeated rationalization toward moral disengagement and emotional distancing.

Research contribution

The framework treats rationalization as an ongoing developmental mechanism rather than only an after-the-fact justification.

Preparing real shelter data

Our team cleaned and preprocessed years of Austin Animal Center records, handled missing values and date fields, explored age, sex and breed patterns, and created variables suitable for modeling. I worked directly on reducing the shelter's many breed labels into meaningful analytical categories and translating those rules into a Python mapping structure.

Interpreting the model

We evaluated logistic regression with recall, precision, specificity and F1—not accuracy alone—and connected the results to practical animal-welfare recommendations around older dogs, spay/neuter status and reducing overemphasis on breed.

Why this one feels very me

It combines a technical modeling problem with animal welfare. The cleaning and feature-engineering decisions felt consequential rather than procedural because the data represented real shelter outcomes.

Psychology Capstone · Experimental Research

Reinforcing the Untrainable

Can an ABA-based positive-reinforcement intervention increase trained behaviors in a domestic cat?

KISSES' RESULTSA tiny research participant with opinions.
Stay70%
Back-up54%
Scratch0%

Baseline average: 0% → Intervention average across behaviors: 41%

DesignSingle-subject AB experiment
Timeline10-day baseline + 24-day intervention
BehaviorsStay · back-up · scratch

Designing the study

For my Psychology empirical research capstone, I designed and carried out a repeated-measures behavioral experiment with my cat, Kisses. I operationalized correct responses, pseudo-randomized cue order, collected daily data and used cooperative-care principles so participation remained voluntary.

Results that were not neat

Overall correct responses increased from a 0% baseline average to 41% during intervention. Stay averaged 70% correct and back-up 54%, while scratch remained at 0%. Instead of hiding the unsuccessful outcome, I investigated what made scratching different—including intrinsic and competing reinforcement.

My biggest takeaway

Real research data is not always pretty, consistent, or expected. An unexpected result is a reason to investigate, not a reason to force the original hypothesis.

Research ethics in practice

When Kisses developed a serious UTI during the study, I paused data collection for her welfare. A clean dataset never matters more than the subject behind it.

From class prototype to a problem I actually care about

The original class concept centralized pet-care tracking, reminders, appointments and records. I kept developing it because the underlying problem became much more concrete: individual measurements are less useful without context, history and patterns.

Where the concept is going

The newer wireframes add customizable health trackers, chronological events, respiratory-rate logging, water and litter-box trends, context filters, combined measurements and pattern detection. The goal is not to replace veterinary judgment; it is to make the owner's observations more structured and easier to interpret and share.

Product principle

Smarter data should make care feel clearer—not create another dashboard you have to babysit.
