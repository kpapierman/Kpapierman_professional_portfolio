Machine Learning · Data Mining

Predicting Controllable Flight Delays

How can airlines identify flights at higher risk for delays they may actually be able to influence?

Data2024 U.S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics; domestic departures from North Carolina
MethodsDecision Tree · Random Forest · Logistic Regression · XGBoost · SHAP
FocusClassification, class imbalance, model evaluation, explainability

The problem

My team narrowed a national flight dataset to North Carolina departures and created a target focused on controllable delays—carrier, NAS congestion, and security—rather than weather and late-arriving-aircraft delays. We reduced the original feature set, engineered variables around departure time, weekends, carriers and airports, and prepared categorical features for modeling.

What made the analysis interesting

The delayed-flight class was imbalanced. Models could look strong on overall accuracy while doing a poor job identifying the flights we actually cared about. We compared balanced/scaled and unbalanced configurations and evaluated recall, balanced accuracy and F1 alongside accuracy. Scaled XGBoost produced the strongest overall balance among the models we tested.

Analytical judgment

A high accuracy score is not automatically a useful model. The evaluation metric has to match the decision the model is supposed to support.

From model to decision

We used SHAP to interpret predictions rather than treating the model as a black box. Departure hour emerged as the strongest predictor, with carrier, week of year, weekends/holidays and Charlotte Douglas International Airport also contributing. We recommended using risk flags proactively in scheduling and resource allocation and identified real-time operational variables—such as turnaround time and ground-crew staffing—that could strengthen future modeling.

02

Independent Research · SURF

Parallel Paths to Crime: How Internal Traits Shape Fraud Motivation

Why do people facing similar opportunities and pressures make very different choices about occupational fraud?

RoleFirst author; research under Dr. Ifeoma Udeh, Accounting
ApproachIntegrative interdisciplinary literature review and conceptual model development
FieldsForensic accounting · cognitive psychology · behavioral ethics · criminology · social psychology

From class idea to independent research

The project began in a Financial Crimes capstone, where I became interested in a limitation of traditional fraud frameworks: opportunity and pressure help explain circumstances, but not why people in similar circumstances respond differently. I developed the idea further through independent research and presented the resulting first-authored poster at Elon's Student Undergraduate Research Forum.

The framework

I synthesized theoretical literature, empirical qualitative and quantitative studies, offender accounts and case evidence around recurring psychological stages. The resulting dual-path framework proposes that people may enter fraud through different internal routes: one involving cognitive dissonance and rationalization when moral identity is relatively intact, and another in which predispositional traits or weaker moral identity allow earlier moral disengagement. The paths converge through repeated rationalization toward moral disengagement, emotional distancing and emotional numbing.

Research contribution

The framework treats rationalization as an ongoing developmental mechanism rather than only an after-the-fact justification, while viewing opportunity as an amplifier of internal processes.

What I learned

This project taught me how to take an ambiguous question that crosses disciplines, find common mechanisms across very different kinds of evidence, build a defensible conceptual structure, and turn a dense literature base into a visual research story.

03

Machine Learning · Animal Welfare

Austin Animal Center Adoption Patterns

What characteristics are associated with dog adoption outcomes in a large, messy real-world shelter dataset?

Scale173,000+ animal records, 2013–2025
ModelLogistic regression
Performance74.9% accuracy · 83.6% recall · 0.771 F1

Preparing real shelter data

Our team cleaned and preprocessed years of Austin Animal Center records, handled missing values and date fields, explored age, sex and breed patterns, and created variables suitable for modeling. One part I worked on directly was reducing the shelter's many breed labels into meaningful analytical categories and translating those rules into a Python mapping structure.

Interpreting the model

We used logistic regression to examine adoption outcomes and evaluated more than accuracy alone, including recall, precision, specificity and F1. The project connected model output to practical animal-welfare recommendations, including promoting older dogs, considering the strong association between spay/neuter status and adoption, and placing less emphasis on breed alone.

Why this project mattered to me

It combined a technical modeling problem with a domain I genuinely care about. Working with imperfect real-world records made the data-cleaning and feature-engineering decisions feel consequential rather than procedural.

04

Psychology Capstone · Experimental Research

Reinforcing the Untrainable

Can an ABA-based positive-reinforcement intervention increase trained behaviors in a domestic cat?

DesignSingle-subject AB experiment
Timeline10-day baseline + 24-day intervention
BehaviorsStay · back-up · scratch

Designing the study

For my Psychology empirical research capstone, I designed and carried out a repeated-measures behavioral experiment with my cat, Kisses. I operationalized what counted as a correct response, pseudo-randomized cue order, collected data during daily sessions, incorporated checks on recording consistency, and used cooperative-care principles so participation remained voluntary.

Results that were not neat

Overall correct responses increased from a 0% baseline average to 41% during intervention. Stay averaged 70% correct and back-up 54%, while scratch remained at 0%. Rather than hiding the unsuccessful outcome, I used behavioral theory and literature to investigate what made scratching different—including intrinsic reinforcement and competing reinforcement—and considered how the intervention could be redesigned.

My biggest takeaway

Real research data is not always pretty, consistent, or expected. An unexpected result is a reason to investigate, not a reason to force the original hypothesis.

Research ethics in practice

When Kisses developed a serious UTI during the study, I paused data collection for her welfare. That decision became part of how I think about research quality: a clean dataset never matters more than the subject behind it.

05

I/O Psychology · Workplace Behavior

Why Good Employees Do Bad Things

What drives counterproductive work behavior—and what can organizations actually do about it?

Evidence to action

I synthesized organizational-psychology research on individual traits, workplace stressors, organizational justice and ethical climate. Using the Stressor–Emotion Model, I examined how job stressors can contribute to anger and frustration that in turn relate to counterproductive behavior, rather than reducing the problem to simply having “bad employees.”

I translated the research into interventions at the individual, job and organizational levels: emotion regulation and conflict management, clearer roles and balanced workloads, stronger procedural and interpersonal justice, and leadership practices that model fair and ethical norms.

Connection to People Analytics

The project reinforced my interest in understanding workplace outcomes as an interaction between people and systems—and in using evidence to move from “what happened?” to “what could we change?”

06

Strategic Management · CapSim

Team Chester: Competitive Strategy Simulation

What happens when marketing, production, R&D and finance decisions all collide?

Iterative business decisions

In CapSim, my team managed a simulated company across repeated competitive rounds. We made linked decisions across R&D, marketing, production and finance, reviewed the consequences, diagnosed mistakes and adjusted our strategy. Our differentiation-oriented approach included product development, TQM, automation, capacity planning and human-resource investments.

Learning from the rough rounds

Mid-simulation liquidity, capacity and inventory problems forced us to confront how decisions that look reasonable inside one function can create problems elsewhere. We adjusted forecasting, automation, capacity, product positioning and financial management over subsequent rounds. TQM investment produced a reported 68% reduction in R&D cycle time, while competitor analysis highlighted the value of tighter coordination among forecasting, production and R&D.

Strategic lesson

Optimization inside one department is not the same thing as a good business decision. The system matters.
