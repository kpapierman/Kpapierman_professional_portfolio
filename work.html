Machine Learning · Data Mining

Predicting Controllable Flight Delays

How can airlines identify flights at higher risk for delays they may actually be able to influence?

Data2024 U.S. BTS domestic departures from North Carolina
MethodsDecision Tree · Random Forest · Logistic Regression · XGBoost · SHAP
FocusClassification · Class imbalance · Explainability

The question

My team wanted to make a huge flight dataset more useful than simply predicting whether any delay would happen. We created a target for delays airlines may have more ability to influence, including carrier, National Aviation System congestion and security delays, while excluding weather and late arriving aircraft delays.

What made it interesting

The delayed flight class was imbalanced, which meant a model could have high accuracy while barely identifying the flights we actually cared about. We compared recall, balanced accuracy and F1 alongside accuracy and tested balanced and unbalanced versions of multiple models. Scaled XGBoost gave us the strongest overall balance for identifying controllable delays.

What I took from it

A high accuracy score does not automatically mean a model is useful. The metric has to match the problem you are trying to solve.

From model to decision

We used SHAP to understand what was driving predictions instead of leaving the final model as a black box. Departure hour was the strongest predictor, with carrier, time of year, weekends and holidays, and airport variables also contributing. We connected those findings to proactive scheduling and resource planning.

Independent Research · SURF

Parallel Paths to Fraud: How Internal Traits Shape Financial Crime

Why do people facing similar opportunities and pressures make very different choices about occupational fraud?

RoleFirst author · Faculty mentor: Dr. Ifeoma Udeh
ApproachInterdisciplinary literature review · Conceptual model
FieldsForensic Accounting · Psychology · Behavioral Ethics · Criminology

From a class idea to independent research

This started with a question from my Financial Crimes capstone. Traditional fraud frameworks explain circumstances such as pressure and opportunity, but I was more interested in why people in similar circumstances can make completely different choices. I kept following that question after the class ended and developed it into independent research.

The framework

I reviewed work across forensic accounting, cognitive psychology, behavioral ethics, criminology and social psychology. The model proposes two internal paths into fraud depending on moral identity. Both paths can move through repeated rationalization toward greater moral disengagement, emotional distancing and eventually emotional numbing.

What I was trying to add

I treated rationalization as part of an ongoing psychological process rather than only something that happens after a person has already committed fraud.

03

Machine Learning · Animal Welfare

Austin Animal Center Adoption Patterns

What characteristics are associated with dog adoption outcomes in a large, messy shelter dataset?

Preparing real shelter data

Our team worked with more than 173,000 Austin Animal Center records spanning multiple years. We cleaned missing values and dates, explored patterns in age, sex and breed, and created variables that could actually be used in a model. I worked directly on reducing the shelter's many breed labels into useful analytical categories and translating those rules into Python.

Interpreting the model

We used logistic regression to model adoption outcomes and evaluated the model with recall, precision, specificity and F1 rather than relying only on accuracy. We then connected the results to practical recommendations around older dogs, spay and neuter status, and avoiding too much emphasis on breed alone.

Why I liked this project

It combined the part of analytics I enjoy, cleaning up a messy problem and figuring out how to model it, with animal welfare. The feature engineering choices felt meaningful because every row represented a real shelter animal and outcome.

04

Psychology Capstone · Experimental Research

Reinforcing the Untrainable

Can positive reinforcement based on Applied Behavior Analysis increase trained behaviors in a domestic cat?

DesignSingle subject AB experiment
Timeline10 day baseline · 24 day intervention
BehaviorsStay · Back up · Scratch

Why I chose it

The project started with a question I genuinely wondered about: why do we assume dogs should be trained but so often treat cats as untrainable? I designed my Psychology empirical research capstone around my cat, Kisses, to test that assumption using Applied Behavior Analysis and positive reinforcement.

Designing the study

I operationalized correct responses, used a single subject AB design, pseudo randomized cue order, collected daily data and used cooperative care principles so Kisses could choose whether to participate. The intervention used clicker and treat pairing across three target behaviors: stay, back up and scratch.

Results that were not perfectly neat

Overall correct responses increased from a 0% baseline average to 41% during intervention. Stay averaged 70% correct and back up averaged 54%, while scratch remained at 0%. Instead of treating that unsuccessful behavior as something to hide, I looked at what made scratching different, including intrinsic and competing reinforcement.

My biggest takeaway

Real research data is not always clean or expected. An unexpected result is a reason to investigate, not a reason to force the original hypothesis.

Research ethics in practice

When Kisses developed a serious UTI during the study, I paused data collection for her welfare. The project made research ethics feel much more concrete because the subject was an animal I knew and cared about.

05

Product · AI · Pet Health

Pawfect Care

A pet health concept built around a question I kept running into in real life: how do you turn scattered daily observations into something actually useful?

From a class prototype to something I actually want to use

The first version was a class project for keeping pet care information in one place, including daily logs, appointments, records and reminders. I kept coming back to the idea because tracking one number at a time is not very helpful when what you really need is context, history and patterns.

Where I want to take it

The newer wireframes add customizable health trackers, a chronological timeline, respiratory rate logging, water and litter box trends, context filters, combined measurements and pattern detection. I also want it to work offline as a PWA so basic tracking is still available when there is no connection.

The idea behind it

I do not want AI to replace veterinary judgment. I want the app to help organize what an owner is already seeing so changes are easier to notice, explain and share with a veterinarian.

06

I/O Psychology · Workplace Behavior

Why Good Employees Do Bad Things

What drives counterproductive work behavior, and what can organizations actually do about it?

I pulled together Industrial and Organizational Psychology research on individual traits, workplace stressors, organizational justice and ethical climate. Using the Stressor Emotion Model, I looked at how emotions and workplace systems can contribute to counterproductive work behavior instead of reducing the problem to simply having bad employees.

Why it connects to my analytics interests

This project reinforced why I like People Analytics. Workplace outcomes are rarely just about the person or just about the system. I am interested in using evidence to understand both and then asking what an organization could actually change.
